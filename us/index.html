How it calculates

Each deposit goes in at the start of the year and grows once a year at your return minus fees. Withdrawals start the year after you stop adding, each one a fixed share of that year's opening balance, taken before growth. IRA limits follow the year of each deposit: $5,500 from 2015, $6,000 from 2019, $6,500 in 2023, $7,000 in 2024 and 2025 and $7,500 from 2026, plus a catch-up of $1,000 from age 50, or $1,100 from 2026. Later years keep the 2026 limits. The limit covers all your IRAs together, and you can’t put in more than you earn. The page assumes your income is low enough to contribute in full: for 2026, contributions start shrinking at $153,000 for single filers and $242,000 for joint filers. Withdrawals are tax-free once you’re 59½ and five years have passed since your first deposit. Earlier ones can owe tax on the growth plus a 10% penalty, which the page doesn’t work out. The regular-account comparison assumes a 1.5% dividend yield and 15% federal tax on dividends and on gains when you sell, and leaves out state tax.
